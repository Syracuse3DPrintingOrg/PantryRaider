# Reducing waste: extending and adjusting best-by dates

A handful of everyday actions help you use food before it spoils, without
re-typing any dates: passing an item the sniff test to keep it a little
longer, marking a package opened so its date follows, and moving food to the
freezer (or back out again) so its best-by date follows the new storage. And
when something does not make it, tossing it keeps the record honest, so the
Waste summary can show you what keeps losing the race.

## The Expiring list

The Expiring page (under Inventory) lists everything within a week of its
best-by date, soonest first, with a colored badge showing how many days are
left or how long ago it lapsed. Each row is the stock with one best-by date in
one place: three yogurts that expired in the fridge and two that are still
good get rows of their own, and the good ones only show up once their own date
comes into the window. From each row you can mark that stock consumed (which
removes it from your stock), toss it when it went bad, print a label for it
when printing is set up, and pass it the sniff test. Every button acts on the
row's own stock and nothing else, so tossing the expired yogurts never takes
the good ones with them.

## The sniff test: keep it a little longer

A date on the package is a guess, and plenty of food is still good past it.
When you have checked an item and it looks and smells fine, the **Sniff test**
buttons on its row push the best-by date out without you editing anything:
**+1d**, **+3d**, or **+5d**. Pantry Raider moves that row's best-by date
forward by that many days (counting from today when the item is already past
its date) and tells you the new date. The same item kept somewhere else, like a
chicken in the freezer good until next year, keeps its own date. The item then drops down the
list, or off it, so it stops nagging you until the new date comes around.

The sniff test only appears where it makes sense. An item whose date is a
hard expiration (a safety date, the way its product is set up in Grocy) never
offers the keep-it-longer buttons, and its row says why instead of leaving a
silent gap; using the item up or tossing it still takes one tap. The sniff
test also meets you on the Review screen: when a scanned item is already
sitting in your stock and about to expire, its review card offers the same
+1, +3, and +5 buttons, so you can deal with the older stock without leaving
the review. The card names the stock it means, for example "3 Greek Yogurt in
the Fridge expired 2 days ago", and its buttons act on just that.

## Toss it: when something did not make it

When an item on the Expiring list is past saving, the toss button beside the
consume check removes it from stock recorded as spoiled instead of eaten. It
asks first, naming how much and which date ("Toss 3 Greek Yogurt (best by
Oct 25)?"), then confirms what happened. The Review screen's sniff-test card
offers the same way out for older stock that fails the sniff, and it tosses
only the stock the card names. The distinction
matters: consumed food fed someone and tossed food did not, and keeping the
two honest is what makes the Waste summary worth reading.

## The Waste summary

Those tosses add up to a Waste card at the bottom of the Expiring page: your
most-tossed items, how many times and how much of each went in the bin, and
what share of everything you used of that item was tossed instead of eaten.
The bag of spinach that keeps losing the race stops getting a pass, and the
next shopping trip can answer for it (the smaller bag, or half into the
freezer on day one).

## Learning from your kitchen

Pantry Raider also learns from what you have already used and thrown out.

**Best-by dates that match your kitchen.** When you mark the same product as
spoiled at least three times, new items of that product get a best-by date
that matches how long it really lasts in your kitchen. If your strawberries
usually go bad after three days, the next box is dated three days out instead
of a week. Learned dates only ever get shorter, never longer, they never
apply to the freezer, and your own expiry rules always win. Printed labels
mark these dates "est." like other estimates. The first few times it shortens
a date, a note on the Pending page says which product and why, with a Turn
off button right there.

**Running low soon.** Next to the restock suggestions on the Shopping page, a
Running low soon list shows items you still have but will probably use up
within a week, based on how fast you have used them over the last two months.
Tap the plus to put one on your shopping list before it runs out.

Both are worked out on your device from your own Grocy history, and nothing
is sent anywhere. Turn them off with **Learn from my kitchen history** under
Settings, Inventory, Learn from your kitchen.

## Mark it opened

Cracking the jar changes the clock: most food keeps far longer sealed than
open. Every in-stock item on the Inventory dashboard has a one-tap **Opened**
button: tap it the moment you open the package and the item's after-opening
shelf life takes over its best-by date, with an Opened badge on the row so a
glance tells you which package is already open. The after-opening shelf life
comes from the product's own setup in Grocy, so a product without one simply
keeps the date it had.

## Every best-by date on a row

Food you bought on different days keeps the best-by date of each purchase. So
when one place holds a product with more than one best-by date, the Inventory
dashboard gives each date a row of its own: two coconut yogurts due in 3 days
and a new pack of 8 due in a month show as a row of 2 and a row of 8, each with
its own badge, instead of 10 yogurts all due in 3 days. The buttons on such a
row only touch the items with that date: Use one, Mark one opened, Mark all
consumed, Move, Print label and a new date in Edit all stay with that row, and
the buttons and their questions say which date they are for. A category change
in Edit still applies to the whole product, and a place where everything has
the same date keeps its one row. Grocy decides for itself which item it takes
next, so once in a while it cannot act on exactly that row's items; the button
then says why in a short note, and nothing changes.

To list each product once per place instead, turn off One row per best-by date
in Settings, Inventory. A row that holds more than one date then gets a short
line under the name listing how many have each date, earliest first, such as
"2 by Oct 24 · 8 by Nov 20". Stock without a date shows as "with no date" and
stock that never expires as "with no expiry". After three dates the line counts
the rest ("+2 more"); point at it with a mouse to see them all. Either way, a
row that never expires says No expiry on its badge, and the choice is the same
on every phone, computer and kitchen screen that uses this server.

## Moving food to the freezer adjusts the date for you

Freezing buys time and thawing spends it, so Pantry Raider shifts the best-by
date when you move an item between storage places that cross that line. On the
Inventory dashboard, drag an item (or use its move menu) from Refrigerated to
Frozen and its date is recomputed from today against the item's frozen shelf
life, which for most food pushes it well out; the confirmation tells you the
new date. Move it back from Frozen to Refrigerated and the date is pulled in to
the shorter refrigerated shelf life instead.

To freeze only some of it, set the Amount at the top of the move menu before
you tap a place. Freezing 4 of 6 chicken breasts moves those 4 with a new date
and leaves the other 2 in the fridge with the date they had. The Amount starts
at the whole row, and dragging always moves all of it.

The adjustment is careful in both directions. Freezing never shortens a date
that is already further out than the freezer shelf life would set, and thawing
never extends one: because the app does not keep a record of the pre-freeze
date, the honest cap on a thaw is the date currently on the item.

Moves that do not cross that line leave the date alone. Shuffling between Room
Temp and Pantry, or into a custom location or Other, never touches an item's
date.

Your own rules still lead. The freeze and thaw shelf lives come from the same
Expiry Defaults the app uses everywhere else, so an expiry rule you have edited
for a product is what a move uses, ahead of the community table and the
built-in rules of thumb (the full order is on
[Community shelf life and privacy](community-shelf-life.md)).

Grocy can set the date too. If you give a product freezing or thawing days in
Grocy itself, Grocy dates it as it goes into or comes out of a place Grocy
knows is a freezer, and the confirmation shows the date Grocy kept. Products
without those days, which includes every product Pantry Raider adds, keep the
dates described above.

### One fridge and one freezer in Grocy

A new Grocy comes with a place called Fridge, and Pantry Raider files fridge
food there. A Freezer that Pantry Raider adds is marked as a freezer in Grocy.

An install set up with an earlier release may have two fridges in Grocy, Fridge and
Refrigerator, and a Freezer that Grocy does not know is a freezer. The Pending
page offers to fix each one, and nothing changes in Grocy until you press its
button:

- **Merge** moves everything in Fridge to Refrigerator with the same amounts,
  best-by dates and opened items, sets products that belonged in Fridge to
  Refrigerator, then hides Fridge in Grocy. Nothing is deleted, and Grocy's
  history keeps its old entries. If the merge is interrupted, press Merge again
  and it carries on from where it stopped. Grocy cannot move a product weighed
  with its container, so the item names any such product; change its location
  in Grocy and press Merge again.
- **Mark as freezer** marks Freezer as a freezer in Grocy and changes nothing
  else.

Either item can be snoozed or archived instead, and once it is done or archived
it does not come back. Only someone who can change Settings sees the buttons.
A place of your own, such as a Garage Fridge, or one a custom storage category
uses, is never merged.
